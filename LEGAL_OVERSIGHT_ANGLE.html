THESIS: BCC is one of the most-audited — and least-overseen — nonprofits in Berks County. It has filed 10 federal single audits (a legal requirement because it spends $750K–$1M+ in federal funds) and been inspected by DDAP, yet no agency has published a single BCC-specific adverse finding, the IRS audit probability is under 0.2%, county monitoring reports are not public, and a compliance violation had to escalate to the PA Superior Court (898 MDA 2017) before any oversight body acted. The question this angle answers: who was legally responsible to catch the documented issues — and why didn't they?

📊 B. The Single-Audit Trail — 10 Audits on Public Record (the strongest new evidence)

EVEN-HANDED NOTE: An absence of published findings is not itself proof of misconduct — agencies don't publish every review. The angle's force is procedural: (1) the single audits exist and were never examined; (2) a court-admitted violation went uncaught by inspection; (3) every trigger was reactive (a lawsuit, an MCO audit, a DDAP complaint) rather than proactive; and (4) the auditor-auditee relationship (Herbein both prepares and audits) removes the independent check.
