⚖️ 2. Decisive Legal Framework — 24 CFR §578.49

HUD Continuum of Care "Leasing" funds cannot be used on grantee-owned property. Under 24 CFR §578.49 and HUD CoC program rules, leasing funds pay rent on units leased from an independent third-party owner under a master or unit lease. HUD explicitly prohibits using leasing funds on units owned by the recipient, subrecipient, parent organization, or related organizations.

CONSEQUENCE FOR BCC: The two grants literally named "BCC Leasing 1" ($203K) and "BCC Leasing 2" ($193K) are funded under the Leasing component. Under the federal rule, the units they fund cannot be owned by BCC — they must be leased from third-party owners. Either BCC leases these units from independent landlords (compliant, and the most likely reading of the component label), or BCC is out of compliance with 24 CFR §578.49 if any leased unit is in fact BCC-owned.

Reference: eCFR Title 24 Part 578 (Continuum of Care Program), HUD Exchange CoC Eligible Activities — Leasing. This regulatory rule is what makes the ownership question legally material: misclassifying owned units under Leasing is a federal grant-compliance violation with recoupment risk.
