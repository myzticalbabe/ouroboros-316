📄 Verified-Findings Body (shared template)

All statements below are supported by public records — IRS Forms 990 (FY2018–FY2025), PA Superior Court 898 MDA 2017 (Justia), HHS OIG A-02-21-02010 & Work Plan SRS-A-26-025, HUD OIG 2023-LA-0002, PA DOS registry, PA DDAP survey database, USAspending (125 federal awards enumerated), ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer.
I am writing to respectfully request a legislative hearing and/or oversight review of Berks Counseling Center (BCC), a 501(c)(3) Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic in Reading, PA (EIN 23-2043478). BCC receives $3.94M in SAMHSA CCBHC funds (grant H79SM088878), derives ~94% of its $12.5M annual revenue from Medicaid/Medicare ($11.78M), and holds 8 HUD Continuum of Care supportive-housing grants (latest-grant sum $1,265,019). A USASpending pagination pull documents 125 federal awards across agencies (SAMHSA, HUD CoC, and others) — the corrected full inventory (an earlier partial pull reported ~50). In total, the organization is supported by more than $10M in public funding per year. The following findings, all from public records, warrant committee review: 1. FIRST OPERATING DEFICIT IN 7 YEARS: FY2025 net income was −$796,924 after years of $1.35M–$1.65M surpluses. Revenue fell 17.6% from its FY2021 peak, yet investment income grew 11.13× ($40K → $446K) in four years while all $11.68M in program spending is reported in a single line item. 2. BOARD-FUNDER-ADVISOR INTERLOCKS: Board Chair Joseph Plageman Jr. and Director Linda Krause both work for Edward Jones while BCC holds ~$10.48M in public securities (portfolio manager undisclosed; Schedule L reports NO related-party transactions). Director Missy Orlando works for United Way of Berks County, which granted BCC ~$685K (incl. $678K MacKenzie Scott funds). Treasurer John Robinson owns Robinson & Associates, LLC (ACTIVE PA entity #3967553). Reading City Councilmember Marcia Goodman-Hinnershitz is Director of Planning at COCA — BCC's largest non-federal funder ($3.3M pass-through). 3. COURT-ADMITTED COMPLIANCE VIOLATION: In Berks Counseling Center v. Community Care Behavioral Health (898 MDA 2017, PA Superior Court), BCC conceded operating without a supervising physician at its Chester Counseling Center satellite — a violation of 55 Pa. Code §1223.52. A $105,486 Medicaid recoupment was cancelled only on procedural grounds (first-audit "provider education" remedy), not on the merits. 4. FEDERAL OVERSIGHT EXPOSURE: HHS OIG (A-02-21-02010, 2023) found SAMHSA failed to verify staffing, level-of-effort, and financial-report compliance for 28 of 30 sampled CCBHC-E grants — BCC's grant is in that population. A live HHS OIG audit series (SRS-A-26-025) is examining whether CCBHCs provided required services for Medicaid payments. HUD OIG (2023-LA-0002) documented $257M in recaptured CoC funds nationally, and BCC's ~$396K in CoC "Leasing" components is subject to 24 CFR §578.49 (no Leasing funds on grantee-owned units). 5. STATE COMPLAINT INVESTIGATION: PA DDAP conducted a complaint investigation at BCC on December 6, 2023 (Event 4V0D11 / Facility TQ8F6601; public portal logs it as telephone); BCC was found in compliance, but the investigation itself underscores the need for proactive oversight. I respectfully request: (1) a committee inquiry into CCBHC governance and conflict-of-interest standards; (2) referral of the governance findings to the PA Attorney General Charitable Trusts & Organizations Section; and (3) review of state pass-through funding oversight for BCC and comparable CCBHCs, including whether county block-grant allocations to BCC are itemized and audited. ⚠️ ALLEGED — complainant account (not independently verified): a PA state parolee with a 7-year PA Dept of Health medical-marijuana certification and a 6-year accepted BCC treatment relationship reports BCC arbitrarily changed treatment requirements to abstinence-only SUD protocols after an algorithm change, enforced under threat of parole arrest. Medical marijuana is legal medicine in PA (35 P.S. §10231.2103(a)); Gass v. 52nd Judicial District (232 A.3d 706, Pa. 2020) bars penalizing registered patients for lawful MMJ use, and PA Cannabis Coalition v. 23rd Judicial District (Pa. 2026) requires case-by-case review. This concerns CCBHC person-centered-care standards and state-licensed SUD provider conduct. HOW TO PROCEED WITHOUT FURTHER INTERVENTION: (1) direct PA DDAP (717-783-8675 · RA-licensuredivision@pa.gov) and PA OMHSAS (717-783-4850) to review BCC's documented clinical justification for an abstinence-only mandate (55 Pa. Code Ch. 1223) and whether the change was algorithm-driven; (2) refer the CCBHC grantee concern to SAMHSA (CCBHC@samhsa.hhs.gov) and HHS OIG; (3) note the 30-day PB-40 administrative-remedy deadline for the affected parolee and the Gagnon I hearing right if a violation is threatened; (4) Disability Rights Pennsylvania (1-800-692-7443) can assess ADA/accommodation. Full memo: MMJ_PAROLE_RIGHTS.html. Full documentation — multi-year 990 data, the Superior Court opinion, OIG report citations, the complete 125-award federal inventory (USASpending pagination), and HUD property records — is available on request.
