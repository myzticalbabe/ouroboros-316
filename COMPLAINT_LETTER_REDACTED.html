COMPLAINT FOR INVESTIGATION AND PRAYER FOR RELIEF — Berks Counseling Center (EIN 23-2043478) — Financial Management, Transparency, Oversight, Federal Grant Compliance, and Governance Concerns — Broadcast to All Oversight Parties

📄 COMPLAINT FOR INVESTIGATION AND PRAYER FOR RELIEF

TO: The Berks County Commissioners, Reading City Council, and ALL CC'ed Oversight Parties Listed Above — Media, Legal, City, County, State, Legislative, and Federal: This is a Complaint for Investigation and Prayer for Relief regarding Berks Counseling Center (BCC, EIN 23-2043478, 645 Penn Street, Reading, PA 19601). BCC leadership is NOT a recipient of this complaint. BCC is the subject of this investigation, not a partner in it. The systemic corruption pattern documented in SYSTEMIC_CORRUPTION_JUSTIFICATION.html — board-funder interlocks, CFO>CEO compensation inversion, undisclosed Treasurer's consulting firm, single-category program spending, and the history of litigating against oversight — requires higher-level intervention. This complaint is sent simultaneously to all oversight parties so that each may investigate within its respective jurisdiction. BCC is not contacted so that the investigation is not compromised. I am writing to formally request review and investigation of Berks Counseling Center (BCC), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization (EIN 23-2043478) located at 645 Penn Street, Reading, PA 19601. As a $12.5 million organization that derives 94% of its revenue from government sources — primarily Medicaid, Medicare, a $3.94 million federal SAMHSA grant, and federal HUD Continuum of Care grants — BCC serves some of the most vulnerable residents of Berks County. With that public funding comes a responsibility for transparency and accountability. After extensive review of BCC's IRS Form 990 filings (FY2018–2025), federal grant databases, county budget documents, and public litigation records, I have identified the following concerns that warrant investigation: 🚩 CONCERN 1: $796,924 Operating Deficit (FY2025) BCC's revenue has declined $1.41 million (10.1%) over two years, while expenses grew $1.04 million (8.5%), flipping a $1.65 million surplus into a $796,924 deficit. This is the first deficit in at least 7 years — prior years (FY2018–2024) showed surpluses of $1.5M to $6M annually. What changed? Is this deficit structural or temporary? 🚩 CONCERN 2: Executive Compensation — CFO Paid More Than CEO (Verified: FY2025 Schedule J / Part VII) CEO Christine Axford and CFO Greg Little compensation figures have been reconciled against the FY2025 Form 990 (Part VII officer-compensation data via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, verified live):
  • CEO Christine Axford: $140,004 base + $5,428 other = $145,432 total (FY2025)
  • CFO Greg Little: $132,442 base + $17,350 other = $149,792 total (FY2025)
The earlier apparent "conflict" was a base-vs-total comparison artifact: prior research reported total compensation (CFO $149,792 > CEO $145,432), while ProPublica's summary panel displayed base compensation only (CEO $140,004 > CFO $132,442). Both sources are correct for what they measure — and on total compensation the CFO is still paid $4,360 more than the CEO, rare for a 501(c)(3). The CFO's "other compensation" ($17,350) is 3.2× the CEO's ($5,428). BCC should still publish the Schedule J detail (compensation methodology and the composition of the CFO's "other compensation") so the inversion and any bonus/benefit components can be explained. 🚩 CONCERN 3: No Program Spending Visibility All $11.68 million in program expenses are reported under a single program category (Program 4a). There is no breakdown between mental health services, substance use disorder treatment, crisis intervention, community outreach, or other programs. This prevents any meaningful assessment of how public funds are allocated. 🚩 CONCERN 4: Above-Average Administrative Overhead Management and general expenses consume 12.0% ($1.59 million) of total spending — above the ~10% benchmark for behavioral health nonprofits. Fundraising is only 0.2%, meaning virtually all non-program spending is administrative. What drives this overhead? 🚩 CONCERN 5: Potential Conflicts of Interest & Undisclosed Financial Relationships - Major donors are REDACTED in BCC's public 990 (Schedule B withheld) - 🦉 Two board members (Joseph Plageman Jr., Chair, and Linda Krause) work for Edward Jones Investments — BCC holds approximately $19.8 million in assets (ProPublica reports up to $22.97M in more recent years) with investment income that grew 11x in 4 years ($40K to $446K). Who manages this portfolio? Are investment contracts competitively bid? - 🦉 Board member Missy Orlando works for United Way of Berks County, which granted BCC $685,000 (including $678K MacKenzie Scott funds). This is a 🦉 cahootery conflict. - Council Member Marcia Goodman-Hinnershitz serves as Director of Planning at COCA (Council on Chemical Abuse), which provides pass-through funding for BCC's substance use disorder programs — a dual oversight/funding role. 🚩 CONCERN 6: Board Treasurer's Consulting Firm — VERIFIED REGISTERED ENTITY (Self-Dealing Risk Upgraded) John Robinson, BCC Board Treasurer since June 2021 (reported $0 compensation), owns ROBINSON & ASSOCIATES, LLC — verified as an ACTIVE Pennsylvania-registered entity (PA Dept of State, Entity ID #3967553, incorporated July 14, 2010, principal address 1742 Colony Drive, Wyomissing PA 19610). BCC's Form 990 Schedule L reports ALL-NO related-party transactions — yet no verification exists that BCC has never contracted with or paid its own Treasurer's consulting firm (financial analysis & strategic planning). The firm's active Commonwealth registration plus the Treasurer's dual role make this a material, confirmable disclosure risk — no longer merely a name-match question. Has BCC ever paid Robinson & Associates, LLC? Was the Treasurer recused from any vendor selection or investment decision? 🚩 CONCERN 7: HUD Continuum of Care Leasing Compliance — 24 CFR §578.49 (Federal Grant Risk) BCC receives approximately $500K–600K+ annually in HUD Continuum of Care (McKinney-Vento) grants for 8 supportive housing properties: 1135/37 Franklin St. ($288K), BCC Leasing 1 ($203K), BCC Leasing 2 ($193K), New Hope PSH ($204K), 13 S. 10th St. ($87K), 239 S. 5th St. ($123K), PS Recovery ($105K), and Phoenix House ($59K). Two of these grants — "BCC Leasing 1" and "BCC Leasing 2" ($396K combined) — are funded under the CoC Leasing component. Federal regulation 24 CFR §578.49 PROHIBITS using CoC Leasing funds on grantee-owned units: leasing funds may only pay rent on units leased from independent third-party owners. BCC must therefore demonstrate these units are third-party leased — or BCC is out of federal grant compliance with recoupment exposure. Notably, BCC's 990 investment portfolio (Schedule D) contains $0 in real estate, and ownership status of the 8 properties has never been disclosed. BCC must produce lease agreements or deed records for all 8 properties. ADDITIONAL FINDING: BCC Litigation History In 2015, BCC's managed care organization (Community Care Behavioral Health) conducted a Fraud, Waste & Abuse audit and attempted to retract $105,486 in Medicaid funds, citing documentation deficiencies including missing supervisory physician signatures. BCC sued and won at the Pennsylvania Superior Court (898 MDA 2017), with $81,704 in retractions cancelled. BCC's defense included a claim that a "software glitch" caused signatures to be hidden rather than missing. ADDITIONAL FINDING: Prior DDAP Regulatory Scrutiny (Dec 6, 2023) On December 6, 2023, the Pennsylvania Department of Drug and Alcohol Programs (DDAP), Bureau of Program Licensure, conducted a complaint investigation of BCC (Event 4V0D11, Facility TQ8F6601) and closed it with BCC found "in compliance" within the scope of that investigation. The investigation's very existence shows BCC has faced prior complaint-driven regulatory scrutiny over its licensed operations. The closure is scope-limited: it does not address the billing substance behind 898 MDA 2017 or any practice after December 6, 2023. (Evidence: DDAP_INVESTIGATION_EVIDENCE.html — public portal record retrieved August 1, 2026.)
