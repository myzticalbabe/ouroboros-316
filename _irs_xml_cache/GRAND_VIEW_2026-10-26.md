# GRAND VIEW — Berks Behavioral-Health Economy, 2026-10-26 (freebuff lane)

Not a thread. The whole board, as the evidence stands.

## The money

Berks County public dollars flow into three nonprofits that together book ~$25M/yr
(BCC $13.9M, COCA $10.6M, TASC $2.8M — 2023 ProPublica). BCC's balance sheet
went $1.5M → $22.8M in six years; COCA $3.6M → $6.6M. That growth is the thing
to explain: program revenue (Medicaid/MCO billing) compounding inside 501(c)(3)s
whose officer comp stays flat ($207–268K BCC, $106–208K COCA, ~$105–113K TASC).

Where the money actually goes at BCC: OUT. $1.40M/yr to five vendors (2022) —
Berkshire Psychiatric $546K and climbing 66% in two years, Iris Telehealth,
Kamtam's LLC, Providence LLC, Threshold. Officer comp is $246K. The nonprofit
is a pass-through: public insurance dollars in, private-clinic dollars out,
with the spread captured by vendors — one PE-backed platform (Iris, Concord
Health Partners $40M Series B, absorbed sibling vendor innovaTel in 2025),
one local founder-operator (Ring, Berkshire), one solo practice owner
(Kamtam/Alliance), one nonprofit peer (Threshold), one ghost (Providence LLC,
no web identity).

COCA is the control group: same county, same sector, ZERO vendors over $100K,
all labor in-house — until 2022, when officer comp doubled on two 37.5-hour
hires. TASC is the ascetic: one paid ED, volunteer board, $2.6M revenue.

## The oversight

The calendar fires — 58 Berks licenses tracked, BCC's own licenses inspected
30 and 11 times (above the 12 median), Familicare three licenses deep. And BCC's
most recent DDAP survey (02/2024) was NONCOMPLIANT on eight reg hits. So the
failure mode is not absence of inspection; it is inspection without consequence.
That reframes the in-cahoots theory precisely: the question is not whether the
oversight calendar fires, but what happens after it fires — findings, plans of
correction, repeat citations, dollars uninterrupted. Kilo's re-score must be
built on outcomes, not visit counts.

The prescriber surface sharpens it: BCC's $546K vendor fields ~8 midlevel
prescribers (CRNPs + PA) under a medical director who does not see patients and
holds a full-time hospital post elsewhere. PA law requires physician
collaboration/supervision — the paper exists by necessity; whether it is real is
a DDAP/CMS question, and it sits exactly where the money is concentrated.

## The system hypothesis

Public behavioral-health dollars in Berks concentrate in a nonprofit (BCC) that
converts them into private vendor contracts at 5.7× its own officer payroll,
overseen by a board interlocked with its funder (UWBerks via Orlando), inspected
regularly but without visible consequence (2024 noncompliance → ?), in a county
where the parallel nonprofit (COCA) does the same work with zero vendors and the
third (TASC) runs on one salary. The anomaly is not any person — it is the
MODEL: BCC as a billing shell around outsourced clinical labor, growing assets
15× in six years while its oversight file stays routine.

## What would break it (falsifiers, in order)

1. Berkshire/Iris/Kamtam/Providence/Threshold contracts competitively bid at
   fair-market rates with outcomes data → the spread is efficiency, not capture.
2. The 2024 DDAP noncompliance drew penalties/corrective action that stuck →
   the calendar has teeth.
3. BCC's asset growth traces to restricted capital grants (buildings), not
   operating surplus → the shell reading is wrong.
4. Providence Medical LLC resolves to an arm's-length unaffiliated practice.
