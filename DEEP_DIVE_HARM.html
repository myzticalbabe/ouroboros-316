🔴 SECTION 1 — PROVEN HARM (Independent Public Records)

HARM 1 — BCC CONCEDED OPERATING WITHOUT A SUPERVISING PHYSICIAN (Patient-Safety Violation)

In the Medicaid Fraud, Waste & Abuse audit underlying Berks Counseling Center v. Community Care Behavioral Health Organization (No. 898 MDA 2017, Pa. Super. Ct., Nov. 7, 2018), BCC conceded it had no supervising physician during the audit period at its Chester Counseling Center satellite — a documented violation of 55 Pa. Code § 1223.52(a)(6)(i), which conditions Medicaid payment on supervisory physician review and sign-off of initial assessments. This is a patient-safety deficiency admitted in a court record, not an allegation.

"At trial, Community Care asserted that the retraction was not merely for a missing signature, but because CCC failed to have a supervising physician at all during the audit period. BCC conceded that it did not have a supervising physician during the audit period."

Source: PA Superior Court memorandum, 898 MDA 2017, via law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/superior-court/2018/898-mda-2017.html. The retraction was cancelled on procedural grounds (first-audit "provider education" remedy under the 2011 FWA policy) — NOT on the merits.

HARM 2 — HHS OIG FOUND SAMHSA FAILED TO ENSURE CCBHC GRANTEE COMPLIANCE (BCC Is a CCBHC-E Grantee)

HHS OIG Report A-02-21-02010 (Sept 11, 2023): For 28 of 30 sampled CCBHC Expansion grants, SAMHSA's award and monitoring procedures failed to verify that clinics (1) filled key personnel positions / met level-of-effort requirements, (2) timely submitted Federal Financial Reports, and (3) properly reported cash on hand. BCC holds CCBHC Expansion grant H79SM088878 ($3.94M) — squarely within the audit's scope population.

HHS OIG Work Plan SRS-A-26-025 (Nov 17, 2025) — LIVE AUDIT SERIES: "Audit of Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics Medicaid Reimbursement and Compliance With Demonstration Requirements" — will evaluate whether states claimed eligible FMAP and whether select CCBHCs actually provided mandatory clinical services. BCC's $11.78M/yr Medicaid program revenue is directly in scope. oig.hhs.gov/reports/work-plan/browse-work-plan-projects/audit-of-certified-community-behavioral-health-clinics-medicaid-reimbursement-and-compliance-with-demonstration-requirements/

HARM 4 — $105,486 IN MEDICAID RECOUPMENT DEMANDED OVER BILLING/DOCUMENTATION DEFICIENCIES

The 2015 Community Care FWA audit demanded retraction of $105,486.13. The trial court cancelled $81,704.47 only because the MCO lacked contractual authority to retract on a first audit cycle — the underlying deficiencies (missing physician sign-offs; no supervising physician) were conceded, not disproven. The alleged "software glitch" hiding signatures was credited by the trial court for the electronic submissions only; the Chester satellite's physician absence stood.

HARM 5 — HUD RECAPTURED $257M FROM CoC GRANTEES — BCC'S LEASING COMPONENT IS A MONITORING GAP

BCC's 8-property CoC portfolio (latest-grant sum $1,265,019; two "Leasing" components ≈ $396K) is subject to 24 CFR §578.49 (no Leasing funds on grantee-owned units) — a rule with weak federal enforcement per this OIG finding.
