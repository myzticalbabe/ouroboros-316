⭐ NEW ANGLE A2 — 42 CFR Part 2: the SUD-confidentiality hook (strongest new compliance lever)

BCC is a federally assisted SUD program (SAMHSA CCBHC grant + Medicaid) — squarely within 42 CFR Part 2. If BCC reported (or threatened to report) a registered MMJ patient's lawful medication use — or their SUD-treatment status — to parole authorities without written patient consent or court order, that is a potential Part 2 disclosure violation with civil and criminal penalties (42 U.S.C. §1320d-5/1320d-6). This matters because: (1) it does not depend on the complainant's credibility — it is a provider-conduct question; (2) SAMHSA is the enforcement agency for Part 2 (SAMHSA, 5600 Fishers Lane, Rockville MD 20857; SAMHSAFOIA@samhsa.hhs.gov); (3) the MMJ/parole coercion allegation can be framed as a confidentiality breach, not just a clinical-policy dispute. Evidence needed: BCC's consent forms, privacy practices notice (Part 2 requires a specific Part 2 notice, not generic HIPAA), and any non-compliance reports to parole referencing SUD treatment or MMJ status.

⭐ NEW ANGLE A3 — Verify actual delivery of all 9 CCBHC service categories

BCC bills Medicaid ~$11.78M/yr as a CCBHC under a state demonstration, which presumes delivery of the 9 required categories. Single-program 990 reporting makes category-level delivery unverifiable. Request: SAMHSA CCBHC certification file (FOIA), the state demonstration MOU between PA DHS and BCC, and Medicaid category-level claims data (PA Right-to-Know / DHS). If any required category is billed but not delivered, that is a Medicaid overpayment / certification-compliance question.
