🌿 YOUR RIGHTS AT A GLANCE

Medical marijuana, treatment, and parole in Pennsylvania — a 1-page plain-language summary
From the full legal memo: MMJ_PAROLE_RIGHTS.html · Session 21, August 1, 2026

✅ The bottom line

In Pennsylvania, using your legally prescribed medical marijuana is not "substance abuse," and you cannot be penalized — by a treatment program, a parole officer, or a court — just for using it lawfully.

If a treatment provider like Berks Counseling Center (BCC) suddenly requires you to stop using your legal medicine — or threatens to report you to parole for using it — that demand is open to challenge under PA law. You have legal protections and clear next steps.

📜 What the law actually says (in plain words)

  • Medical marijuana is legal medicine in PA. The state's own law (the Medical Marijuana Act) treats it as treatment, not as an abused drug. If you have a valid PA Dept of Health card, your use is lawful.
  • You can't be punished "in any manner" for lawful use. The law says a registered patient can't be arrested, penalized, or denied any right or privilege just for using medical marijuana lawfully. That reaches treatment requirements and parole conditions.
  • No blanket bans for people on supervision. PA's Supreme Court ruled unanimously (the Gass case, 2020) that probation/parole cannot impose a blanket ban on medical marijuana. Your lawful use, by itself, is not a violation.
  • Each case must be looked at individually. A 2026 PA Supreme Court follow-up case confirms blanket rules stay invalid — officials can only make reasonable, case-by-case checks that you're using it lawfully.
  • Conditions must be written down. A parole condition is only enforceable if it's actually in your supervision order. A BCC office policy isn't the same as a written parole condition.
  • Treatment plans must be individualized. Providers must document a real clinical reason (medical necessity) for changing a treatment plan — they can't just flip a policy based on an algorithm change.

🤔 Questions BCC must answer if they demand abstinence-only

  • Is the abstinence requirement actually medically right for you, this specific patient?
  • Why did a 6-year accepted relationship suddenly change — what's the written clinical reason (not an algorithm or billing change)?
  • How is lawful medical marijuana "substance abuse" under PA law, when the law says it's medicine?
  • Were you offered any accommodation or a second opinion?
Fair warning (the other side): providers generally may set their own clinical standards, and MMJ can be medically inappropriate in some situations. That's exactly why BCC must be able to document its reason — not just point to a policy change. Don't skip the documentation steps below.

🚨 If a violation is threatened or a warrant issued — act fast

STEP 1 — Demand a hearing immediately. You have the right to a preliminary hearing (called a "Gagnon I" hearing) before any violation can be used against you. Your defense is clear: lawful medical marijuana use is not a violation. Make them prove the use was unlawful (no card, illicit source, over the legal amount) — it wasn't. And if you are ever detained over lawful MMJ use, challenge it in court with a lawyer (habeas corpus) — don't wait.
2
Document everything now. Your certification date, your years of treatment, the written abstinence-only requirement, the algorithm-change notice, any threats or letters. Get copies of your BCC treatment plan and records.
3
File the BCC internal grievance, then escalate to the state licensing agency (DDAP) if it's not resolved.
⚠ PATIENT ACTION — The patient files their own grievance. The investigator does NOT contact BCC.
4
Challenge the parole condition through the PA Board of Pardons administrative remedy — 30-day deadline from the date of the board action.
5
Report the funding concern to SAMHSA (the federal agency that funds the clinic) — a funded provider shouldn't be imposing a non-medically-justified mandate.
6
Get a lawyer — for parole defense and/or a disability-rights (ADA) claim. Free options exist (see contacts below).
