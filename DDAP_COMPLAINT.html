Subject: Licensing Complaint — Berks Counseling Center (Facility ID TQ8F6601, EIN 23-2043478), 645 Penn St, Reading PA — arbitrary abstinence-only treatment-plan change imposed on a registered medical-marijuana patient, with threat of parole arrest To the Bureau of Program Licensure: I am filing this complaint against Berks Counseling Center (BCC), a DDAP-licensed drug and alcohol treatment provider (Facility ID TQ8F6601; EIN 23-2043478) at 645 Penn Street, Reading PA 19601, regarding a treatment-condition practice that I believe raises patient-rights and licensure-standard concerns under 28 Pa. Code Chapter 709 (Standards for Licensure of Freestanding Treatment Facilities) and Chapter 704 (staffing), and treatment-planning standards under 55 Pa. Code Chapter 1223. 1) CONTEXT. BCC is also a Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic (CCBHC) receiving a $3.94M SAMHSA grant (H79SM088878) and roughly $11.78M/yr in Medicaid/Medicare revenue. Your Bureau conducted a complaint investigation at BCC on December 6, 2023 (Event 4V0D11) and found the facility "in compliance" (the public D&A Facility Surveys portal logs the event as a telephone complaint investigation). This complaint asks that the scope of licensing review be expanded to the patient-rights and treatment-condition practices described below. 2) THE PRACTICE AT ISSUE (ALLEGED — single-complainant account, not independently verified). A registered Pennsylvania medical-marijuana patient — a PA state parolee with a 7-year PA Dept of Health medical-marijuana certification and a 6-year accepted treatment relationship with BCC — reports that BCC arbitrarily changed treatment requirements to abstinence-only substance-use-disorder protocols after a treatment-algorithm change, and that compliance was demanded under duress and threat of parole arrest (refusal to comply reported as parole non-compliance → technical violation → warrant). [Date(s)/month-year the requirement change was imposed: ________. Staff who communicated the change: ________.] 3) WHY THIS RAISES LICENSURE CONCERNS. (a) Patient rights (28 Pa. Code Ch. 709, Subch. C, §§709.21–709.34). Medical marijuana is a lawful medicine under PA law, not an abused substance. 35 P.S. §10231.2103(a) provides that a registered patient "shall not be subject to arrest, prosecution, or penalty in any manner, or denied any right or privilege… solely for the lawful use of medical marijuana." The PA Supreme Court (unanimous) in Gass v. 52nd Judicial District, 232 A.3d 706 (Pa. 2020) barred penalizing registered patients for lawful MMJ use and prohibited blanket prohibitions under supervision; PA Cannabis Coalition v. 23rd Judicial District (Pa. 2026) requires case-by-case evaluation; Baldassano (2025 PA Super 26) requires any condition be explicit in the supervision order. An abstinence-only mandate that classifies lawful medication as "substance abuse" and leverages parole-arrest threats to enforce it conflicts with these protections. (b) Individualized treatment planning (55 Pa. Code Ch. 1223). A 6-year accepted relationship followed by a unilateral, allegedly algorithm-driven requirement change requires documented clinical justification and individualized medical-necessity assessment — not a categorical policy shift. (c) Records confidentiality (4 Pa. Code §255.5; 42 CFR Part 2). Threatening to report a patient's SUD-treatment status to parole authorities implies disclosure of substance-use-disorder treatment information to a third party without written patient consent or court order — a potential Part 2 violation (42 U.S.C. §290dd-2; penalties §§1320d-5/1320d-6). 4) SUPPORTING CONTEXT ON RECORD. In Berks Counseling Center v. Community Care Behavioral Health (898 MDA 2017, PA Superior Ct. 2018), BCC conceded operating without a supervising physician at its Chester Counseling Center satellite (55 Pa. Code §1223.52(a)(6)(i)); a $105,486 Medicaid recoupment was cancelled only on procedural grounds, not on the merits. HHS OIG Report A-02-21-02010 (2023) found SAMHSA failed to verify compliance for 28 of 30 sampled CCBHC-E grants — BCC's grant is in that population. 5) REQUESTS. (1) Reopen / expand the licensing review of BCC's treatment-condition practices toward registered medical-marijuana patients under 28 Pa. Code Ch. 709 Subch. C and 55 Pa. Code Ch. 1223. (2) Require BCC to produce the documented clinical justification for any abstinence-only mandate, including whether the requirement change was algorithm-driven, and any individualized medical-necessity assessment for the affected patient. (3) Confirm whether the December 6, 2023 investigation (Event 4V0D11) addressed patient-rights / coercion / treatment-condition concerns, and obtain BCC's response to those issues. (4) Review BCC's client-rights, records-confidentiality (4 Pa. Code §255.5 / 42 CFR Part 2), and grievance procedures, and whether any patient was reported to law-enforcement or parole authorities for lawful medical-marijuana use. HOW TO PROCEED WITHOUT FURTHER INTERVENTION: The affected patient will provide treatment-history documentation directly to your intake channel under appropriate consent and Part 2-compliant authorization. BCC's public filings (990s, the Superior Court opinion, DDAP event history) are identified above and available on request. Please initiate the standard complaint intake and risk-triage, contact the complainant through your intake process with their written authorization, and notify BCC of any documentation requests. No further input from me is required to begin review. Thank you for your consideration. Sincerely, [Your Name] [Your Contact] [Date]
