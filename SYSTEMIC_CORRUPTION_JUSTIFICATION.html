🚫 Why BCC Leadership CANNOT Be Contacted

The decision to NOT contact BCC leadership is not a courtesy — it is an investigatory necessity. The following reasons justify this position:

1. Tipping Off the Subject Destroys the Investigation: Alerting BCC before oversight bodies act gives them time to coordinate responses, align board members, retain counsel, and potentially destroy or alter documents. Every board member with a conflict of interest would be warned before any investigator asks a single question.
2. The Pattern Is Self-Protecting: The board governs itself. The board chair's employer may manage the portfolio. The treasurer's consulting firm is undisclosed. The funder sits on the board. This is not a system that will self-correct when asked politely — it is a system designed to resist external scrutiny. Asking BCC to investigate itself is an invitation to cover-up.
3. BCC Already Demonstrated Its Response to Oversight: When Community Care Behavioral Health found $105,486 in documentation deficiencies, BCC sued and won — on procedural grounds, never addressing the underlying signature question. The "software glitch" explanation was accepted by a court but never independently verified. BCC's response to scrutiny is litigation, not transparency.
4. The COI Policy Has Never Been Requested: The single most important document — the Conflict of Interest policy — is legally required to be provided upon request (IRC §6104(d)(1)(A)). It has never been requested. Once requested, BCC would have the opportunity to review, revise, or limit what it provides BEFORE any oversight body reviews it. The COI policy should be subpoenaed or requested by an oversight agency with enforcement authority — not by a private complainant.
5. Complainant Safety (CONCERN 8): A PA state parolee with a 7-year MMJ certification reports being coerced into abstinence-only protocols under threat of parole arrest. BCC leadership — if contacted — could identify this complainant from the narrow profile (parolee + MMJ + 6-year BCC relationship). Contacting BCC creates a retaliation risk against a vulnerable patient. The redacted complaint exists specifically to prevent this.
6. Oversight Bodies Have the Authority BCC Lacks: The Pennsylvania Attorney General can subpoena. SAMHSA can audit. PA DDAP can investigate licensing. The County Commissioners control the human services block grant. City Council controls local policy. Each of these bodies has enforcement power that a private complainant does not. They should be the ones asking questions — and they should be asking them simultaneously, without warning.
7. The CC List Itself Is the Leverage: Sending the complaint to ALL oversight parties simultaneously — media, legal, city, county, state, legislative, federal — ensures that no single agency can bury it. The CC list demonstrates to every recipient that every other oversight body has been notified. This is the structural counter to BCC's structural opacity.
