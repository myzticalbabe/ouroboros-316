📋 STRATEGY OVERVIEW

Berks Counseling Center Investigation — EIN 23-2043478, Reading, PA
Executive Summary for Journalists, Legislators & Regulators · August 1, 2026

This is a systemic corruption investigation.
BCC is the subject — not a partner.

Berks Counseling Center, a $12.5M/year behavioral health nonprofit deriving 94% of its revenue from government sources, exhibits a self-reinforcing system of board-funder interlocks, financial opacity, and governance failures that requires higher-level intervention. BCC leadership is not contacted — all complaints go to oversight bodies with enforcement authority.

STRATEGY Why BCC Is NOT Contacted

1. Alerting BCC destroys the investigation. Tipping off the subjects before oversight bodies act gives them time to coordinate responses, align board members, and potentially alter or destroy documents.
2. The pattern is self-protecting. The board chair's employer may manage the portfolio. The treasurer owns an undisclosed consulting firm. The funder sits on the board. This is a system designed to resist scrutiny — not one that will self-correct when asked.
3. BCC already litigated against oversight. When Community Care Behavioral Health found $105,486 in Medicaid documentation deficiencies, BCC sued and won (898 MDA 2017) — on procedural grounds, never addressing the underlying issues. BCC's response to scrutiny is litigation, not transparency.
4. The COI policy has never been requested. The single most important governance document — the Conflict of Interest policy required under IRC §6104(d) — should be obtained by an oversight body with enforcement authority, not a private complainant. Once BCC is asked, they can review, revise, or limit what they provide.
5. Complainant safety. A PA state parolee with a 7-year MMJ certification reports being coerced into abstinence-only protocols under threat of arrest. Contacting BCC risks retaliation against this vulnerable patient.
6. Oversight bodies have the authority BCC lacks. The PA Attorney General can subpoena. SAMHSA can audit. PA DDAP can investigate licensing. These bodies should ask the questions — simultaneously, without warning.

GAPS 5 Critical Gaps — What's Still Missing

42 of 49 investigation gaps remain open. These 5 block action:

1. COI Policy — Never Obtained. BCC is legally required to provide it under IRC §6104(d). The request letter exists but has not been routed through an oversight body. Without the COI policy, the board-funder interlock questions cannot be resolved.
2. Is Edward Jones Managing the Portfolio? — Unconfirmed. The $10.48M securities portfolio's manager is never named in any public filing. Two board members (22%) work at Edward Jones. This is the central question of the entire investigation.
3. No Complaint Filed — Zero Contacts Made. Despite 20 action items, no oversight body has been contacted. Commissioners, City Council, SAMHSA, PA AG, OMHSAS — all contact info compiled, none used. The investigation has external effect only after delivery.
4. FY2026 Form 990 — Not Yet Available. The latest 990 covers FY2025 (filed May 2026). FY2026 data won't be available until ~May 2027. The $797K FY2025 deficit may have worsened or improved — unknown.
5. Schedule B (Major Donors) — Structurally Redacted. BCC e-files its 990s — Schedule B is structurally excluded from public XML by IRS policy. Only paths: direct request from BCC (not advised), IRS FOIA Form 4506-A, or PA RTK for county contracts naming donors.
