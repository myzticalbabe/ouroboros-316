Subject: 42 CFR Part 2 Confidentiality Complaint — Berks Counseling Center (EIN 23-2043478), 645 Penn St, Reading PA — potential unauthorized disclosure of SUD-treatment / medical-marijuana status to parole authorities; requests for BCC's Part 2 privacy notice, consent forms, and parole non-compliance reports To SAMHSA — Part 2 Compliance: I am filing this complaint under 42 U.S.C. §290dd-2 and 42 CFR Part 2 (Confidentiality of Substance Use Disorder Patient Records) regarding Berks Counseling Center (BCC), Reading PA (EIN 23-2043478), a federally assisted substance use disorder program squarely within Part 2's coverage (42 CFR §2.12(b)): BCC holds SAMHSA CCBHC Expansion Grant H79SM088878 ($3,944,396) and derives roughly $11.78M/yr in Medicare/Medicaid program-service revenue. This filing is the Part 2 confidentiality track; the CCBHC grant-compliance track (key personnel, FFRs, certification file) is filed separately to the same office. 1) THE ALLEGATION (ALLEGED — single-complainant account, not independently verified). A registered Pennsylvania medical-marijuana patient — a PA state parolee with a 7-year PA Dept of Health medical-marijuana certification and a 6-year accepted treatment relationship with BCC — reports that BCC imposed abstinence-only SUD protocols after a treatment-algorithm change and threatened to report non-compliance to parole authorities, which would disclose the patient's SUD-treatment status (and/or medical-marijuana status) to a third party without written patient consent or court order. [Fill: date(s) of the requirement change and any threat, and the names of BCC staff involved, if known.] 2) WHY THIS RAISES A PART 2 QUESTION REGARDLESS OF THE UNDERLYING DISPUTE. (a) 42 CFR §2.13 prohibits a program from disclosing any records or information that identifies a patient as having, or having been treated for, an SUD — including disclosures made under threat or coercion — unless a Part 2 exception applies. Disclosure to a parole/probation office is not among the non-consent exceptions (42 CFR §§2.51–2.65: medical emergencies, audit/evaluation, child-abuse reporting, court orders under §2.61–§2.63, and narrow law-enforcement inquiries under §2.65). It requires the patient's written consent under 42 CFR §2.31 — a specific Part 2 consent form that names the recipient, purpose, extent, and expiration, and that can be revoked. (b) Part 2 requires a specific Part 2 privacy notice to each patient (42 CFR §2.22). A generic HIPAA notice does not satisfy Part 2. Whether BCC provides a compliant Part 2 notice is a documented, verifiable compliance question independent of any individual's account. (c) Penalties: knowing and willful violations are subject to criminal and civil penalties — the direct Part 2 misuse penalty (42 U.S.C. §290dd-2(f)) and the HIPAA penalty provisions (42 U.S.C. §§1320d-5/1320d-6). Under the CARA amendments (2020), HHS OCR shares authority to investigate and enforce Part 2; SAMHSA is the agency charged with Part 2 oversight and programmatic compliance. (d) Context on record: BCC is also a CCBHC whose grant (H79SM088878) sits inside HHS OIG Report A-02-21-02010's population (SAMHSA failed to verify compliance for 28 of 30 sampled CCBHC-E grants), with the live OIG audit series SRS-A-26-025 examining CCBHC Medicaid compliance. The PA Medical Marijuana Act (35 P.S. §10231.2103(a)) protects registered patients from penalty "in any manner" for lawful MMJ use, and the PA Supreme Court in Gass v. 52nd Judicial District, 232 A.3d 706 (Pa. 2020) barred penalizing registered patients for lawful MMJ use. 3) REQUESTS — the documentary record needed to assess compliance: (1) BCC's Part 2 privacy notice as provided to patients (42 CFR §2.22 compliance check — not a generic HIPAA notice). (2) BCC's written consent forms for the affected patient's treatment relationship, and the form's compliance with 42 CFR §2.31 (named recipient — e.g., any specific parole/probation office — purpose, extent, expiration, revocability). A consent form that does not name the parole office as recipient cannot authorize disclosure to it. (3) Any non-compliance reports or correspondence to parole authorities referencing SUD treatment or MMJ status, with the legal authority cited for each disclosure (written consent / court order / statutory exception). (4) Confirmation of whether any disclosure to parole/probation occurred for any patient since BCC's treatment-algorithm change, and BCC's disclosure log. (5) Corrective-action review: Part 2 training for clinical staff, policy revision, and an assurance of no retaliation against any patient who asserts Part 2 rights. (6) FOIA: BCC's Part 2 compliance records and CCBHC certification file (cite FAIN H79SM088878) for the grant-compliance context. HOW TO PROCEED WITHOUT FURTHER INTERVENTION: The affected patient will provide their Part 2-protected records and a Part 2-compliant written authorization directly to your intake channel — no identifying or clinical material is contained in this filing. BCC's public filings (IRS 990s, the Superior Court opinion in 898 MDA 2017, DDAP event 4V0D11, USAspending award data — 125 federal awards) are identified in the attachments and available on request. Please initiate Part 2 complaint intake, request the documents in §3 from BCC, and coordinate with HHS OCR / HHS OIG as appropriate. No further input from me is required to begin review.
